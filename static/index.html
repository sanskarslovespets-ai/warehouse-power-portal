Pendency is a snapshot of shipments with no terminal status yet, taken at the refresh time above — it ages by the hour, unlike the breach views. Breached = promise timestamp already passed. Due today / tomorrow = promise falls on that date. All in IST. D1 / D2 / D3-7 / D8+ = calendar days past the promise date. Promise timestamp uses the same dual rule as the breach views: stored Edd_Delivery_time for whole-day Edd_Sent, else order_date + ROUND(Edd_Sent × 86400) sec. Terminal statuses (Delivered, RTO-Delivered, Cancelled, Lost, Damaged, NotServiceable) are excluded, as are in-flight RTO-* shipments — those are heading back to origin, so the customer promise no longer applies. Only shipments created in the last 60 days are counted; older open records are overwhelmingly stale rows that were never closed out. Shipments with no EDD cannot be bucketed and are excluded.

Invoice Entry

One row per line item of each uploaded PDF invoice, under the warehouse its Ship To address names (by the warehouse's pincode, or the city). The invoice number opens the PDF in Google Drive. An invoice is read only if its lines add up to its Sub Total and, with tax, to its Total; one already here isn't added (or uploaded) again. Total amount = the line's amount before tax plus its tax (IGST, or CGST and SGST). SKU used = the ERP's completed delivery notes (not returns) from the invoice's warehouse that carry the box's SKU, dated on or after the invoice date: one delivery note is one box. SKU left = Quantity minus SKU used. When a warehouse has more than one invoice for a SKU, boxes count against the oldest invoice first, so none is counted twice.

By courier

Source CX_data.CDC_Nugget, one row per ticket, joined to cp_database_N on AWB for the courier column. Scoped to 17 issue / sub-issue pairs covering last-mile faults only: cancellation, delay, failed attempts, MDND, damage and expiry. Missing items and wrong item sent are deliberately excluded as packing/content faults rather than delivery faults. Everything outside those pairs is excluded, so this is not total complaint volume. Courier is attributed in two hops: awb_number matched directly against ClickPost, and where that fails and the value is a DN number, resolved through tabDelivery Note.custom_awb_number back into ClickPost. About 81% of tickets in this scope get a courier; the rest show as Unmapped, mostly because the AWB field contains free text or multiple references. order_id is not used - it is not unique. (blank) is a real value, not an error: category, issue and sub-issue are empty strings on roughly 61-67% of tickets in the source table. Ticket volume jumped about fourfold in Jul 2026; that discontinuity is unexplained and may be an ingestion change rather than a real surge, so trend lines across it are unsafe. Fake attempt rate divides Fake Attempt (Nobody Came) tickets by that courier's delivered shipments over the same dates, so couriers of different size are comparable. It is a floor, not an exact rate: tickets whose courier could not be attributed are excluded from the numerator but the denominator is complete, so every courier's true rate is somewhat higher. Tickets are counted by creation date and deliveries by delivery date, which will not line up exactly for any single day. All timestamps IST.

Event basis. Every shipment is counted on the date it reached a terminal outcome — the rto_mark_date for those that came back, the delivery_date for those that did not — so RTO % = RTOs marked in the period ÷ (RTOs marked + delivered in the period). Shipments still in flight, cancelled, lost or damaged sit on neither side of that ratio and are excluded. Unlike the cohort basis this replaced, recent days are not understated: both sides are settled outcomes. Avg days to mark is rto_mark_date − created_at, a lag measure only.
Returns received at warehouse is the one section on a different clock. It counts RTO-Delivered shipments on rto_delivery_date — the day the parcel physically arrived back — because that is what a warehouse reconciles against. The gap is real: the median mark-to-receipt lag is about four days and only ~21% arrive within a day, so these daily counts will not line up with the mark-date views above, and are not meant to. The cumulative column answers "how much had landed by D−1".
Window is the last 100 days. Delhivery Reverse is excluded throughout. Destinations outside the top 80 by volume are bucketed as Other. Value columns show ClickPost invoice_value (tax inclusive) and the ERP net_total (pre-tax); net is blank where the AWB has no matching delivery note.

Connected means origin_hub_out_timestamp — the shipment leaving the origin hub on a line-haul connection. Note the column is origin hub out: cp_database_N has no "origin city out" field, and this is its only origin-side departure timestamp. Fill is 99% on Bluedart and 94% on Delhivery, and it never precedes pickup_date on any row.
The SLA is an absolute morning cutoff, not an elapsed-time budget. A shipment picked on day D should be connected by 08:00 on D+1. So Day 1 is met, Day 2 is one day late (out by 08:00 on D+2), Day 3+ is later than that, and Not connected has no timestamp at all. That means Day 1 is a ~32-hour window for a midnight pickup and an 8-hour one for a 23:59 pickup — deliberately, because that is how the commitment is stated.
Bluedart and Delhivery only. The SDD/NDD network couriers deliver out of the same city and run no line-haul connection, so the measure does not apply to them.
Counts are per bucket; the percentages are cumulative. Day 1 % is Day 1 alone, By day 2 % is Day 1 + Day 2, and By day 3+ % is everything that ever got a connection scan — so that last figure falls short of 100% by exactly the Not connected share. All three divide by total shipments including the unscanned ones: dividing by scanned-only would flatter precisely the warehouses with the worst scan coverage.
The newest pickup dates are immature and marked provisional: a shipment picked today cannot yet have missed an 08:00-tomorrow deadline, so those rows always look worse than they will settle at. Window is the last 60 days of pickups.
Export CSV gives one row per shipment for the dates and filters above, whichever table you export from (at most 92 days at a time).

What is timed: destination_hub_in_scan_time to out_for_delivery_1st_attempt — the shipment landing at the destination hub, then going out for delivery. OFD coverage is 99% on Bluedart and 98% on Delhivery.
"RAD" here is NOT the RAD basis on the breach tab. There it means rider_reached_drop_location_first, the rider at the customer's door. Here it means arrival at the destination hub, a different and much earlier event. Do not read the two as the same measure.
Same day or next day, counted in calendar days — deliberately unlike the Connection tab, which scores against an absolute 08:00 cutoff. Same day is OFD on the arrival date, Next day the day after, D+2 or later beyond that, and No OFD has no first-attempt timestamp at all. Percentages are cumulative and divide by total shipments, so by next day % falls short of 100% by the D+2 and No OFD shares combined.
Destination hub comes from each courier's own mapping on drop pincode: Bluedart's CSCRCDDESC (12,468 pincodes, none of which map to two hubs), Delhivery's Dispatch Center — the centre that puts shipments out, not incoming_center, which differs on 12.3% of pincodes. 0.6% of shipments land on a pincode absent from both files and show as Unmapped.
Seven mother warehouses, Bluedart and Delhivery only. Window is the last 30 days of arrivals.
